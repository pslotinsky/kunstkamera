# Kingdom HR — Ideas

**Status:** CONCEPT

A working list of game ideas and possible approaches, not a technical specification or a set of finalized requirements. Words such as "may" and "could" indicate unresolved choices. Tags may be used to group ideas later; for now, all ideas remain in a single list.

## I-01. Résumé Review
`#recruitment` `#ui`

The player sits at a desk and carefully examines candidates' résumés, work experience, references, and other information. Inconsistencies and clues to hidden traits may be worth investigating. Applications can be discarded, set aside, or selected for an interview.

## I-02. Job Interviews
`#recruitment` `#dialogue` `#events`

Before an appointment, the player can interview a candidate. The chosen questions and answers affect what the player learns about them and may have consequences later. After an interview, the player can hire the candidate immediately or ask them to wait. Interviewed candidates can be hired at any time while they remain available.

## I-03. Staff Development
`#personnel` `#progression`

Promising employees can be developed from juniors, trained, and promoted. Being good at a job does not necessarily mean being good at managing other people.

## I-04. Talent Market
`#recruitment` `#world`

Specialists can be recruited from the open market or poached from rival kingdoms. Different candidates may have their own conditions for accepting an offer.

## I-05. Regional Candidates
`#recruitment` `#world` `#geography`

Some people live in specific provinces and become available after those provinces join the kingdom. Whether there should also be other ways to recruit them remains open.

## I-06. Appointments and Dismissals
`#personnel` `#simulation`

The player assigns leaders to positions, assesses their results, transfers them, promotes them, or dismisses them. Personnel changes affect how the kingdom develops but do not give the player direct control over the leaders' actions.

## I-07. Employee Relationships
`#characters` `#relationships` `#events`

Friendship, dislike, rivalry, and personal connections can affect cooperation and give rise to conflicts. Strong individual specialists do not necessarily make a strong team.

## I-08. Burnout and Corruption
`#personnel` `#characters` `#events`

Employees may become overworked, abuse their positions, or pursue personal interests. The player must notice problems and decide how to respond.

## I-09. One-on-One Meetings
`#personnel` `#dialogue` `#narrative`

Personal meetings help the player get to know employees and uncover their histories and problems. Dialogue choices can influence relationships and later events. Employee meetings and candidate interviews share a limited daily allowance.

## I-10. Events as Consequences of Hiring
`#narrative` `#events`

Hiring a particular candidate opens up particular story possibilities. Events may depend on the person's position, background, relationships, and the player's past choices, rather than simply occurring at random.

## I-11. Procedurally Generated Characters
`#characters` `#generation` `#replayability`

Some or most candidates could be procedurally generated, including their backgrounds, skills, personalities, and connections. This could provide variety without requiring every applicant to be written by hand.

## I-12. Narrative Archetypes
`#characters` `#generation` `#narrative`

Authored, reusable narrative roles — rebel, saint, genius, fugitive, spy, and others — could be assigned to suitable characters. How strongly an archetype determines events, and how much the outcome depends on the player's choices, remains open.

## I-13. Unique Destinies
`#characters` `#narrative` `#fate` `#rare`

Unlike reusable narrative archetypes, a destiny is a rare, exceptional background or calling belonging to a particular character. Someone might be descended from a goddess or destined to discover a one-of-a-kind magical tome. Such destinies could give a character a special place in the world and unlock unique events. They might be assigned at random to suitable generated characters; the exact approach remains undecided.

## I-14. Handcrafted Characters
`#characters` `#narrative` `#content`

An alternative or complement to full procedural generation is a small cast of fully authored characters, perhaps tied to particular kingdoms or provinces. The right balance between these approaches has not been decided.

## I-15. Hints in Subsequent Playthroughs
`#replayability` `#narrative`

On subsequent playthroughs, the game could warn players about possible consequences of decisions they have encountered before. If narrative roles are reassigned between runs, such hints would need to account for that variability.

## I-16. Kingdoms as Employers
`#world` `#replayability`

At the beginning of a campaign, the player can take a job in different kingdoms. Each offers a different situation, talent market, set of typical events, and working environment.

## I-17. Autonomous Kingdom Simulation
`#simulation` `#core`

Appointed leaders independently wage wars, develop the economy and science, govern territories, and conduct diplomacy. The player influences outcomes by choosing people rather than issuing direct orders. Direct control of armies was considered as an alternative, but is not part of the core concept. Each turn represents one day; ending the turn advances life in all kingdoms automatically.

## I-18. Job Vacancies and Applications
`#recruitment` `#core`

The player creates and publishes vacancies, specifying a specialty and minimum competencies in that specialty. Applications arrive gradually over subsequent days; some days may bring none.

## I-19. Candidate Waiting
`#recruitment` `#relationships`

Candidates have hidden, individual willingness to wait: some may wait indefinitely, while others leave after a few days. The player sees only how long each candidate has already waited. Candidates may accept another kingdom's offer or pursue something else. Prolonged lack of a response almost guarantees a worsening attitude toward the state; some candidates may hold a grudge with later consequences. Delaying a decision after an interview has more severe consequences than delaying résumé review.

## I-20. Daily News and Staff Reports
`#simulation` `#personnel` `#ui`

At the start of each day, the player receives news and work reports about the previous day. News covers events such as provinces gained or lost, rebellions, and epidemics. Staff reports cover changes in competencies and morale, with individual reports when relevant: level gains, intentions to resign over working conditions, fights, or discoveries of ancient artifacts.

## I-21. Indirect Performance Assessment
`#personnel` `#simulation` `#ui`

The game does not reveal each employee's individual contribution. The player can compare economic, scientific, healthcare, and other charts with hiring dates, and consider personal events, changes in mood and attitude toward the state, and opinions from subordinates and superiors.

## I-22. Kingdom Victory
`#world` `#progression`

The goal is the kingdom's victory over the others. Victory may be military, economic, cultural, or take another form. Provinces of other kingdoms might voluntarily ask to join. The initial version can focus on military victory.


## I-23. Employment Reference Checks
`#recruitment` `#characters` `#investigation`

The player can follow up on employers and references listed in a candidate's Experience section to learn more about their background. Feedback from previous workplaces may clarify their employment history and reveal information or traits omitted from the résumé.

## I-24. Position Strengths and Problems
`#recruitment` `#personnel` `#ui`

Each position can show a summary of the current strengths and problems in its area of responsibility, without recommending whom to hire. The player can use this context to find candidates capable of addressing several existing problems and give lower priority to specialists whose strongest fields are already doing well.
