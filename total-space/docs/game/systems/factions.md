# Factions

**Status:** DRAFT

The final game contains multiple major playable factions with materially different strategic identities.

## Required Faction Definition

Every playable faction must define:

- **Fantasy** — one-sentence identity.
- **Strategic identity** — how it prefers to expand and win.
- **Economy** — how territorial development differs from the baseline.
- **Military doctrine** — characteristic fleet composition and behavior.
- **Expansion model** — how it gains, develops, and holds territory.
- **Unique mechanics** — rules unavailable to or meaningfully different from other factions.
- **Structural weaknesses** — constraints that create counterplay, not merely small numeric penalties.
- **Content identity** — characteristic ships, buildings, technologies, characters, and/or resources.

## Balance Model

Faction balance is evaluated at two levels:

1. **Structural balance** — strengths create different strategic opportunities and have meaningful constraints or counters.
2. **Numerical balance** — costs, stats, rates, and probabilities are tuned after the structural design works.

No faction should accidentally invalidate geography, economy, fleet positioning, or territorial warfare. Exceptions must be deliberate parts of that faction's design.


## Prototype

- Four playable factions use identical rules and content.
- Additional neutral non-playable factions own the planets not assigned to playable factions.
- Neutral factions only develop and defend their own planets: they build, upgrade Settlements, and rely on their Garrisons. They have no Fleets and never attack.
- Faction asymmetry is not required for the prototype.
