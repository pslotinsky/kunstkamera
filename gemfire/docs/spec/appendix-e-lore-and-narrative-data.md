# Appendix E — Lore and Narrative Data

## Purpose

This appendix records confirmed narrative information about Gemfire.

It focuses on the story premise, mythic background, the Gemfire crown, King Eselred, Princess Robyn, Pastha, and the few characters or events that have explicit narrative relevance in available sources.

This appendix avoids speculative house ideology, invented character psychology, and empty profiles.

---

## E.1 Source Scope

The original sources provide a compact narrative frame rather than a full lore bible.

Reliable narrative data is mostly limited to:

- the origin of Gemfire;
- King Eselred’s tyranny or corruption;
- Princess Robyn’s removal of the Gems;
- the scattering of the Gems;
- Pastha and the mythic origin of the crown;
- short scenario prologues;
- a small number of named character events.

When a character or house has no confirmed narrative information beyond gameplay data, it is omitted.

## E.3 Ishmeria

Ishmeria is framed as a small island kingdom at the end of the seven seas.

It is a fairy-tale fantasy land where fairies, monsters, forests, sorcery, noble houses, and magical authority all coexist.

The war for Ishmeria is not only a territorial conflict. It is also a struggle over the Gemfire crown, the magical artifact associated with rule over the kingdom.

---

## E.4 Origin of Gemfire

Gemfire was originally created as a magical seal.

In the mythic background, an evil sorcerer named **Zemmel** arrived on a Fire Dragon and set the land ablaze. **Pastha**, a protective sea/water dragon, doused the flames, and the **King** called upon his six wizards and sorceresses to seal the dragon away.

As the last spell was cast, Zemmel cursed them: the six magicians became six jewels in a ring around the dragon, which turned into a ruby. The king set all seven gems into a crown.

The crown became known as Gemfire / Royal Blood and came to symbolize royal authority over Ishmeria.

---

## E.5 Pastha

Pastha belongs to the oldest mythic layer of the setting.

Pastha is described as a protective, peace-aligned sea/water dragon who doused the Fire Dragon's flames. (It is the **King**, not Pastha, who summons the six wizards to seal the dragon — see §E.4.)

Pastha is therefore not merely a special battlefield unit. Narratively, Pastha is connected to the origin of Gemfire itself.

---

## E.6 The Gemfire Crown

Gemfire is a crown in which seven magical Gems were inlaid: six wizard Gems, which Princess Robyn loosed, and the Dragon's ruby, the one she never got out (see §7.1).
Each guardian's gemstone and element:

| Guardian | Gemstone | Element            |
| -------- | -------- | ------------------ |
| Pluvius  | Sapphire | stars / fireballs  |
| Zendor   | Emerald  | lightning          |
| Empyron  | Garnet   | blue fire          |
| Scylla   | Topaz    | wind               |
| Chylla   | Amethyst | ice                |
| Skulryk  | Opal     | poison cloud       |
| Dragon   | Ruby     | the Fire Dragon    |

The six wizard Gems can leave the crown and choose worthy rulers.

The Dragon remains tied to the crown and to Eselred’s power.

This makes the Dragon different from the six wizard Gems: it is not part of the rebellion’s magical redistribution, but the remaining core of royal power.

---

## E.7 King Eselred

King Eselred is the ruler of Ishmeria and the head of Lankshire.

In the English Gemfire framing, he is presented as a tyrant who rules Ishmeria with an iron fist through the power of the Gemfire crown.

The Royal Blood story translation gives a slightly more nuanced version: the excessive power of the crown corrupts or taints the heart of the king and drives him into madness.

Both versions agree on the important narrative point: Eselred’s rule becomes oppressive because of Gemfire’s power.

---

## E.8 Princess Robyn

Princess Robyn is Eselred’s daughter.

She removes the six wizard Gems from the Gemfire crown to stop her father’s abuse of power.

In the Royal Blood version, she hears Pastha’s voice calling for the wizard jewels to be freed from the crown.

After Robyn removes the Gems, they scatter across Ishmeria and seek worthy rulers.

Eselred discovers her action and imprisons her.

Robyn is the inciting character of the war: without her, Eselred would retain his monopoly over Gemfire.

---

## E.9 Scattering of the Gems

After Robyn removes the six wizard Gems, they scatter across Ishmeria.

The Gems choose or come into the hands of noble rulers.

This breaks Eselred’s monopoly on magical authority and creates a multi-sided civil war.

The conflict is not simply rebels against the king. The noble houses also compete with each other to reunite Gemfire and rule Ishmeria.

---

## E.10 Confirmed Character Narrative Notes

Only characters with explicit narrative information are included here.

### Erin

Erin is one of the major noble claimants after the Gems scatter.

Royal Blood’s translated intro frames Erin as a claimant marked by hardship but still connected to hope.

Erin is associated with Zendor / Thunders.

### Ander

Ander, called Redwald in Royal Blood materials, is another major claimant.

He is framed as Erin’s rival among the strongest noble contenders.

He is associated with Pluvius / Meteo.

### Garth

Garth is one of the few characters with an explicit personality note in the translated story material.

Chylla / Chilla describes him as clumsy-looking but pure-hearted.

### Terian

Terian / Tirian breaks away from the king’s control and forms his own house.

Later scenario material says he destroys Molbrew and takes its territory.

His personal ideology is not explained, but his narrative role as a breakaway royal-aligned figure is explicit.

### Eadric

Eadric / Edrick appears in the late-war narrative as a ruler connected to rumors of royal blood.

Scenario material presents him as Eselred’s long-lost son or possible royal heir.

This makes him a late-war claimant whose legitimacy is tied to the Lankshire bloodline.

### Pender

Pender / Pendragon betrays Erin and joins the king before the final scenario.

This betrayal is treated as strategically serious because of Pender’s skill.

### Loryn

Loryn turns against the king and forms Divas / Diahs in the final scenario.

No clear motivation is provided.

## E.12 Known Narrative Limits

The available sources do not provide a full character bible.

There is not enough reliable information to describe:

- the ideology of each noble house;
- the personal beliefs of most rulers;
- detailed motivations for most characters;
- internal cultures of provinces or regions;
- moral alignment of individual Gems;
- long-term fate of Princess Robyn;
- what happens after Gemfire is reunited.

The confirmed lore is mostly a mythic premise and a short civil-war timeline, not a fully developed political drama.

---

## Summary

Gemfire’s narrative is thin but functional.

Its core story is about a magical crown whose excessive power corrupts royal rule, a princess who breaks that monopoly by scattering the Gems, and a civil war among noble houses seeking to reunite Ishmeria and Gemfire.

The strongest confirmed narrative elements are:

- Gemfire as a crown and magical seal;
- Pastha and the six wizards;
- Eselred’s tyranny or corruption;
- Robyn’s removal of the Gems;
- the scattering of magical authority across noble houses;
- the Dragon remaining with the king;
- a few explicit betrayals and succession hooks in later scenarios.
